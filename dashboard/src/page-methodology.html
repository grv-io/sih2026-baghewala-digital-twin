Scope

The twin simulates one cyclic-steam-stimulation (CSS) cycle on a single heavy-oil producer, well BGW-07 in the Baghewala field, day by day, from the first tonne of steam injected to the day the oil rate falls through the economic cutoff. It is a coupled four-model reduced-order simulator, not a reservoir simulator: there is no grid, no saturation tracking, no multiphase flow and no geomechanics.

Four physical models run in series each day. A thermal model gives the effective temperature of the drainage volume; a viscosity correlation converts that temperature into an in-situ oil viscosity; an inflow-performance relationship converts viscosity into a reservoir-deliverable oil rate; and a lumped rod-pump model decides how much of that rate the pumpjack can actually lift, at what rod load, at what energy cost, and at what risk of rod floating.

Everything downstream — the steam/oil ratio, the fuel and carbon figures, the machine-learning surrogate and the optimiser recommendation — is a function of those four models and of params/field_params.json. Nothing in this product is a measured production number.

Heated-zone growth — Marx–Langenheim, with sensible + latent heat (physics v3, rev 7)

Physics rev 5 (26 Sep). Retained heat now credits the sensible heat of the liquid fraction as well as the latent heat of the steam quality fraction, and passes through a wellbore heat-loss and sandface-quality multiplier before use (effective delivered-heat multiplier ≈ 0.52 at the reference set-points). The 8 m fixed drainage-disc assumption below is superseded 26 Sep: the heated radius this section computes now feeds a composite-radial inflow model (see Inflow performance) instead of a fixed disc, and the 20-day cooldown constant is superseded by a Boberg–Lantz cooldown.

Physics v3 (rev 7, 27 Sep). BL_DELTA_FACTOR = 0.5 is now SOURCED, not a free calibration knob: it is the ½ in Boberg & Lantz's own δ = (1/2Q)∫Q̇p dt (SPE Petroleum Engineering Handbook Vol. V ch. 15, Eqs. 15.70/15.73, reproducing Boberg & Lantz 1966) — and it is also the only value that conserves energy when there is no conduction (θ = 1 − 2δ must equal 1 − Qp/Q). The unsourced blended liquid heat capacity (CP_LIQUID_JM3K) is deleted: δ's produced-heat term now follows Eq. 15.74 stream by stream — oil heat capacity from API gravity plus Gambill's co, water from the steam-table enthalpy difference hf(T̄) − hf(TR). Steam and gas terms are zero, an explicit [ASSUMPTION]. Effect at the reference cycle: SOR −1.5% (4.089 → 4.027); every margin moved more, because the well is thin-margin (+1.5% oil ≈ +19% margin). At the time (rev 7) the remaining δ uncertainty lived entirely in the then-constant fluid.water_cut. rev 11/12 superseded that: water cut is now a STATE (condensate flowback blending toward a formation value as the tank drains), so the uncertainty now lives in fluid.formation_water_cut and fluid.condensate_recovery_frac instead (UQ drivers #3 and #2 respectively; #1 is now the cold well's own skin factor) — see docs/model-improvement/BL_DELTA_FACTOR_SOURCE.md for the original derivation.

Steam injected into a reservoir bounded above and below by conductive layers grows an approximately isothermal hot zone whose growth rate falls as more heat leaks into the over- and underburden. Marx & Langenheim (1959) give that behaviour through a dimensionless heat-loss function.

F(tD) = etD · erfc(√tD) + 2√(tD/π) − 1
Eh(tD) = F(tD) / tD , tD = 4αt / h2 Eh is the fraction of injected heat retained in the zone (→ 1 as tD→0, decaying as heat loss dominates); α = k/(ρcp) = 2.5 / 2.3×106 m²/s is the thermal diffusivity; h = 12 m is the reservoir thickness.

Retained heat is converted to a heated volume, then to a heated radius, then to an effective temperature:

Qret = ṁt · x · L · Eh , Vh = Qret / (ρcp ΔT) , rh = √(Vh / πh)
Teff = T0 + (Ts − T0) · min(Ah/Ad, 1) x = 0.65 steam quality, L = 1.3×106 J/kg latent heat, Ts = 290 °C, T0 = 50 °C, Ad = π(8 m)² the assumed drainage disc.

After injection stops, the zone temperature relaxes exponentially:

T(t) = T0 + (Tpeak − T0) · e−(t−tinj)/τ , τ = 20 d

Assumptions carried in code

  • Over/underburden diffusivity approximated by the reservoir rock's own α — no separate caprock properties exist in the parameter file.
  • Superseded 26 Sep. Only the latent heat of the quality fraction is credited; sensible heat of the liquid fraction and wellbore losses are neglected. Rev 5 credits sensible heat and applies a wellbore heat-loss / sandface-quality multiplier (params wellbore.heat_loss_frac_per_1000m = 0.18, quality_at_sandface_frac_of_wellhead = 0.55, both typical, not Baghewala-measured).
  • Superseded 26 Sep. Drainage radius fixed at 8 m. Rev 5 uses a 100 m composite-radial drainage radius (reservoir.drainage_radius_m) feeding the composite-radial inflow model instead of a fixed disc — see Inflow performance.
  • Produced fluid draws from a drainage-area-weighted blend of hot zone and cold reservoir rather than a sharp isothermal front.
  • Superseded 26 Sep, sourced 27 Sep. The 20-day cooldown constant is a lumped fit, not a Marx–Langenheim result. Rev 5 replaces the fixed τ with a Boberg–Lantz cooldown; physics v3 (rev 7) then SOURCED BL_DELTA_FACTOR = 0.5 as the ½ in Boberg & Lantz's own δ (PEH Eqs. 15.70/15.73) and the only energy-conserving value with no conduction — no longer a free/unverified constant (1.0 would double-count the produced heat).

Viscosity–temperature — Walther / ASTM D341 (Andrade superseded 26 Sep)

Physics rev 5 (26 Sep). The twin now uses the two-constant Walther / ASTM D341 kinematic-viscosity correlation (walther_A, walther_B in field_params.json, fitted from two calibration points at run time and kept null in the file for the same reason andrade_A/B are — writing rounded values back breaks the reference-point-recovery test on float drift). The Andrade form below is superseded as the default and is reachable only via explicit andrade_A/andrade_B_K so benchmark A1 can compare the two laws directly.

Heavy-oil deliverability in CSS is almost entirely a viscosity story: at reservoir temperature this crude is 11,500 cP and effectively immobile; heated to 200 °C it is a few cP. The classical two-parameter Andrade form (superseded) is shown here for reference:

μ(T) = A · eB/TK A and B are solved exactly through two (T, μ) points, since lnμ is linear in 1/TK: B = ln(μ1/μ2) / (1/T1 − 1/T2), A = μ1/eB/T1.

The two calibration points are the field reference (11,500 cP at 50 °C, from field_params.json) and a high-temperature anchor of 50 cP at 150 °C. Because B > 0 for any pair where the hotter point is less viscous, monotonic decrease with temperature holds analytically — which is what the corresponding physics test asserts.

Assumption

  • The 150 °C / 50 cP anchor is a literature-typical value for a crude of this API gravity, not a Baghewala lab measurement. andrade_A and andrade_B_K are null in the parameter file; supplying lab-fit values makes this anchor unused.

Inflow performance — composite-radial inflow (Vogel + fixed AOF superseded 26 Sep)

Physics rev 5 (26 Sep). The fixed AOFref = 0.7 m³/d stand-in below is superseded: twin/ipr.py's composite-radial inflow now scales qmax with a Vogel-style qmax = J·Pr/1.8 that is itself live in reservoir pressure (Pres/PCAL), and multiplies it by a composite-radial skin/uplift ratio built from the heated radius (heated_radius_m) against the 100 m drainage radius. Reservoir pressure itself is no longer held fixed at 11.4 MPa: it charges linearly during injection (+steam_t × a boost constant with no physical anchor, halved to 1.0 during calibration) and bleeds exponentially afterwards (twin/cycle.py's _p_res_at(day)), which is what makes the depletion sweep respond at all — before rev 5 it was inert. ipr.composite_uplift returns exactly 1.0 when the heated radius is absent (cold-well reference case), and MAX_UPLIFT = 10 only bounds a test case; it never binds at field settings (observed max ≈4.5).

Vogel's solution-gas-drive IPR gives the shape of the deliverability curve (still the base form, now composite-radial-scaled rather than a fixed AOF):

q / qmax = 1 − 0.2 (pwf/pr) − 0.8 (pwf/pr)2
qmax = AOFref · (μref / μ) AOFref = 0.7 m³/d is the absolute open-flow potential at the cold reference viscosity; the mobility ratio μref/μ is what couples the thermal state to the oil rate.

At reservoir temperature the well delivers well under 1 m³/d — below every cutoff in the design range. That is the physical premise of CSS for this crude: the well does not flow economically until it is heated.

Assumptions

  • Superseded 26 Sep. No measured productivity index exists in the parameter file; AOFref = 0.7 m³/d is a calibrated stand-in. Rev 5 still has no measured productivity index, but AOF is now derived from qmax = J·Pr/1.8 times the composite-radial ratio rather than a single fixed number.
  • Mobility scales linearly as μref/μ with permeability held fixed.
  • Superseded 26 Sep. Reservoir pressure is held at its initial 11.4 MPa; there is no material-balance depletion. Rev 5 charges Pres during injection and bleeds it exponentially after (T1-C); depletion now moves SOR (test-benchmarked, though the magnitude over-responds vs the Liaohe D3 analogue — see Validation status).

Rod-pump loads and the floating index

Physics rev 5 (26 Sep). Two additions since v1: (1) the pump now runs on a liquid basis — produced rate is converted to total liquid via water_cut = 0.85 (constant) superseded rev 11/12: the day's ACTUAL produced-stream water cut from the water-cut STATE model (condensate flowback blending toward fluid.formation_water_cut = 0.45 as the tank drains, typically 0.87 → 0.55–0.63 over a cycle) before it is compared against pump capacity and before its energy is charged against the cooling heated zone (a higher water cut makes producing harder cool the well faster); (2) SPM is a declining schedule, not a single number — the pump speed you set is a ceiling, bounded day by day by the rods' drag-limited fall velocity (srp.max_spm_for_viscosity), and never below a 2.0 spm floor. Days on which the pump, not the reservoir, sets the rate are counted separately (pump_limited_days).

A lumped single-point simplification of the API RP 11L polished-rod load calculation, extended with a viscous-drag term so the model can express the operating failure mode that matters in heavy oil: rod floating.

Wb = mrodLg (1 − ρf/ρsteel) , Ffluid = ρfgLAp
Fvisc = K · μ · v̄ · L , v̄ = 2 · stroke · SPM / 60
PRLpeak = Wb + Ffluid + Fvisc , FI = min(Fvisc/Wb, 1) L = 1,150 m rod length, mrod = 3.8 kg/m, Ap from a 44.5 mm plunger, ρf from 15.5° API via SG = 141.5/(131.5 + API), K = 10 the lumped drag coefficient.

FI is the ratio of downstroke viscous drag to buoyant rod weight. Above roughly 0.6 the rods cannot fall freely under gravity, lag the polished rod and slap down — compression, buckling, and eventually a parted rod string. That threshold is what alarm ALM-02 on the twin console watches, and what the optimiser's classifier constrains.

Ed = PRLpeak · stroke · SPM · 1440 / 3.6×106 , qpump = Ap · stroke · SPM · 1440 · ηv ηv = 0.85 volumetric efficiency; produced rate = min(qIPR, qpump).

Assumptions

  • Static, one-number-per-day approximation. No rod elasticity, no wave propagation, no dynamic loading — this is not a design tool and the dynamometer card on the console is a shape, not a measurement.
  • K = 10 is a Couette-like lumped coefficient tuned so cold heavy oil at high SPM enters the >0.6 floating band; no annular-gap geometry exists in the parameter file.
  • Pump setting depth approximated by rod length.
  • Energy uses the peak load for every stroke rather than the area under the load–displacement curve.

How the four models make a cycle

twin/cycle.py steps one day at a time on a single clock that runs from the start of injection. Each day: thermal → viscosity → IPR → rod pump.

PhaseDurationWhat runsWhat is recorded
Injectsteam_t ÷ 74 t/dZone growth; steam accrues; well shut inT, μ, cumulative steam, generation energy
Soaksoak_daysExponential cooldown; well still shut inT, μ
Produceuntil q < cutoffCooldown continues; Vogel rate; pump lifts min(IPR, capacity)q, rod load, FI, pumping energy

Cycle summary quantities are then simple aggregations:

SOR = Σsteam / Σoil , e = Σenergy / Σoil , FImax = maxdays FI
margin = revenue − steam cost − fixed cost per cycle , margin/cycle-day = margin / days_total headline SOR stays GROSS; margin/cycle-day shown here is also gross — the optimiser's actual objective since rev 9 is the INCREMENTAL margin/cycle-day (oil over the cold, unstimulated well for the same window, net of daily opex), see Fuel/cost/carbon basis and Surrogate and search.

Corrected 26 Sep. Under rev 5/9 physics, SOR rises monotonically with steam volume (test_SOR_rises_monotonically_with_steam_volume) — more steam always costs more per barrel recovered, honestly, because the heated zone eventually saturates the drainage radius while steam keeps being charged to the numerator. That means "minimise SOR" always pushes to the steam floor and is not a useful objective. Margin per cycle-day is what has the interior optimum instead (test_margin_has_interior_optimum_over_steam_volume): it amortises the fixed per-cycle cost over the inject-and-soak days that earn nothing, so a modest, well-utilised cycle beats both a too-small one (steam wasted heating rock that never gets tapped) and a too-large one (steam cost outrunning the extra oil). That interior optimum in margin is what the ML optimiser now searches for.

Fuel, cost and carbon basis

Baghewala's steam generators are diesel-fired. This is the single most consequential economic fact about the field and it changes the answer by a factor of about six and a half.

Oil India's own operations deck for the BGW-08 first cycle (12 July 2025) records approximately 220 kg/hr of high-speed diesel against approximately 3,100 kg/hr of steam. There is no gas supply to the field — it is a remote Thar Desert location with no pipeline out; the crude itself leaves by road tanker. Everything below follows from that ratio.

HSD per tonne of steam = 220 / 3.1 = 71 kg = 85.5 L
Fuel energy = 71 × 42.6 MJ/kg = 3.02 GJ / t steam
CO2 = 3.02 GJ × 74.1 kgCO2/GJ = 223.8 kg / t steam 3.02 GJ/t sits inside the published 2.6–4.0 GJ/t band for steam generation, and the CO2 figure cross-checks against 71 kg × 3.17 kgCO2/kg diesel = 225 kg to within 1%.

The iso-oil counterfactual

rev 13: the recommendation still injects less steam in absolute terms than the baseline — 1,000 t against 1,300 t — as well as recovering more oil per tonne (a better SOR), so a plain difference of the two fuel bills already understates the real efficiency gain. The honest, and larger, comparison is still how much steam the current (worse-SOR) practice would have needed to make the recommended cycle's oil:

steamcounterfactual = oilopt · SORbase , steamavoided = steamcounterfactual − steamopt

Per cycle, never per year

The twin's days_total is the length of the simulated producing cycle, 167–203 days for the baseline and recommended set-points on this page (these cycles run months, not weeks — test_produce_phase_is_months_not_weeks). It is not the interval between CSS jobs on a well. Published CSS cycles are re-visited on a 6–18 month rhythm; Baghewala banked 39 cycles in roughly 6.5 years across a 34-well field. Dividing 365 by days_total manufactures about 6.8 cycles per well per year — a rate no CSS operation on earth achieves — and any rupee figure resting on it is inflated by roughly the same factor. This product therefore quotes per optimised cycle only. Field-scale figures are a separate, explicitly labelled multiplication by the 19 CSS jobs Oil India ran in FY2025-26, a number the audience can check independently.

Gross vs. incremental — and why it matters (Economics v2, rev 8/9)

Every SOR figure on this site — the headline number, the benchmark comparisons — is gross: steam divided by all the oil the well makes, the same convention every published benchmark this product compares against uses (CalGEM field SOR, Cold Lake's “SOR ≈ 4”, the CSS life-cycle “SOR ≈ 6”). But a Baghewala well left entirely alone — no steam, just pumped cold — still makes oil: at the reference and recommendation set-points it would make roughly 85–125 m³ over the same calendar window (inject and soak days included, since the well is shut in for stimulation and forgoes that cold oil). Gross margin credits CSS with all of that oil, including the oil the well would have made anyway. Incremental margin — oil over that cold, unstimulated counterfactual, net of daily opex (including the cold well's own pumping power) — is the number that actually answers “does stimulating this well beat leaving it alone?”, and it is what the optimiser now maximises and what every ₹ figure on the overview and recommendation pages leads with.

marginincremental = (revenuestimulated − steam cost − rig cost − opex − power) − (revenuecold − opexcold − powercold) same calendar window both sides, under the SAME float policy; rev 13: the cold well is shut in (counterfactual cash = 0) when its OWN floating index, at the policy floor, exceeds the alarm line — a mechanical fact, not an economic one.

rev 13: the FAIR comparison is now the SAME-POLICY gain — the recommendation vs the published-practice baseline (b), BOTH operated VFD-hold. At OIL's CONFIRMED FY25 realisation that gain is +₹3,332/cycle-day (baseline +₹12,064 → recommendation +₹15,396, net cash), driven mostly by the shorter stroke (57%) letting the VFD hold the float line longer, with the cutoff change second (30%). If the baseline is instead assumed to pull at the first alarm (the rev-12 comparison), the gain reads +₹12,917/cycle-day — but 68% of THAT number is the operating-rule switch itself, not the set-point, which is why it is no longer the headline. At the $65 FY26-floor deck the fair gain is +₹4,319/cycle-day; net of royalty and cess, +₹5,382/cycle-day (the recommendation's edge GROWS on the levies deck, because it saves steam). See docs/model-improvement/TIER1_PROGRESS_LOG.md §12.6.

Cold-well vs shut-in: two honest counterfactuals (resolved in rev 13)

The INCREMENTAL margin formula above nets the stimulated well's economics against a cold, unstimulated well producing for the same calendar window, under the SAME float policy. At Baghewala's ~45% formation water cut the cold stream is a water-in-oil emulsion of roughly 51,000 cP (Pal–Rhodes, published band), which drives the Mills dynamic polished-rod load to 189 kN against the assumed 86-in unit's 113.9 kN rating — so at the policy floor its own floating index is 1.0, above the 0.6 alarm line. rev 13: the model now applies the SAME consequence to the cold well that it applies to the stimulated well under that policy: it is shut in (counterfactual cash = 0), not "unpumpable yet producing" (the rev-12 double standard — now a resolved, passing test, test_cold_well_pumpable_at_2_spm_on_the_assumed_unit). Every absolute incremental ₹ figure in this product is therefore still an UPPER bound: Baghewala's wells WERE produced cold historically at a few bbl/d, so a genuinely pumpable cold well (slowed to ~0.53 spm, as the field did) is also carried in the model as the pumpable counterfactual convention.

Two readings of “does CSS beat doing nothing” are both worth showing — and the earlier draft's counterfactual statistic itself needed fixing (see the Uncertainty section's own resolution note above): the robust, always-correct reading is the SAME-POLICY paired win-rate; the recommendation's own P(net cash > 0) (no cold-well counterfactual subtracted at all — "vs a shut-in well") is shown labelled as such, never silently swapped for the incremental figure.

Data lineage

Nothing shown in this product came from a sensor. The chain from parameter file to on-screen recommendation is five steps, all reproducible from the repository.

01Field parametersparams/field_params.json rev 5 (recalibrated 26 Sep 2026) — merged from the OIL internal presentation, SPE-23APOG-535203 and GEOHORIZONS 2015.
02Physics twintwin/{thermal,viscosity,ipr,srp,cycle}.py — the four models above, one day per row.
03Design of experimentstwin/generate_data.py — 3,000 Latin-hypercube samples over the four set-points, each run through the rev-5 twin, seed 42, trained 26 Sep 2026.
04Surrogatesml/train.py — XGBoost regressor on log(oil), XGBoost regressor on margin/cycle-day, XGBoost classifier on floating risk. 80/20 split, seed 42, single holdout (no cross-validation).
05Searchml/optimize.py — skopt gp_minimize, 60 calls, maximising predicted INCREMENTAL margin/cycle-day (rev 9 — was: maximise gross margin/cycle-day at rev 5, minimise predicted SOR before that) with soak fixed at 10 d, subject to p(float) ≤ 0.30. rev 9: when the raw surrogate argmax and the true-physics grid optimum disagreed by more than ~₹300/cycle-day, the physics-grid point was used as the canonical recommendation instead — see the recommendation page's own honesty note.

The dashboard closes the loop: the winning set-points are snapped to the controller grid and put back through step 2, so the number quoted to a GM is a physics result, not a surrogate prediction.

Surrogate and search

The surrogate exists because the twin is too slow to sit inside a 60-call Bayesian loop and far too slow to sit behind a slider. It is a compression of the twin, and it is only as good as the R² and MAE above — both of which are reported on the optimiser page next to every prediction, and both of which measure agreement with the twin, not with Baghewala.

Uncertainty

Every number elsewhere on this page and product is a point estimate at one set of judgement-call values (water cut, net-pay thickness, the diesel bulk discount, the oil price, daily opex, and so on — BL_DELTA_FACTOR is no longer one of them; physics v3 sourced it as a fixed physical constant). ml/uq.py asks the honest follow-up question: how much does the answer move if those judgement calls are varied over their own defensible ranges, instead of held fixed?

A paired Monte Carlo: every one of 1,500 draws (seed 42) samples one joint realisation of 21 uncertain inputs (a selection is in the table below) and runs the reference cycle, the assumed baseline (b) and the recommendation — each under the VFD-hold policy, plus the same-policy pull/none variants — through that same realisation, via the true physics twin (twin.cycle.simulate_css_cycle + summary, not the surrogate). Pairing is what makes a probability like “P(recommended better than baseline)” meaningful: it compares the two set-point choices under the SAME assumed physics on each draw, rather than mixing independently-noisy runs.

Field view: which well gets steam next (demo, 12 synthetic wells)

PS SIH26120 asks for field-level optimisation, not just one well: Baghewala's ~33 operational wells share ONE steam generator (~74 t/d, the 2018 CSS pilot's own injection rate) — the field's bottleneck. ml/schedule.py runs every well's own physics optimum (ml/recommend_physics.py, FI ≤ 0.6 feasibility), then decides which well gets the generator next, how much steam, and when, to maximise the field's total incremental margin over a one-year horizon.

Demonstration on 12 SYNTHETIC wells (BGW-S01…S12, data/external/field_wells_synthetic.csv, seed 42) — NOT real Baghewala per-well data (data/external/SOURCE.md). Three schedules are computed: an exact schedule (bitmask-subset search × earliest-release-date order — provably optimal, see ml/schedule.py), a fast greedy heuristic, and a naive counterfactual (the same fixed 1,500 t for every well, worst-producer-first order, no per-well tuning). Only the exact schedule is charted below; all three are compared in the KPIs and in ml/README.md.

rev 12 (float-onset produce-end rule): the naive, no-per-well-tuning policy now LOSES money field-wide (negative ₹/cycle-day at several of its fixed-set-point wells) — greedy and exact, which tune steam/cutoff/spm per well, turn the field profitable. Say so; a scheduler demo that only shows the upside is not honest about what per-well tuning is buying.

    Validation status

    263 property-based physics and published-benchmark tests run under pytest (265 collected), plus two declared, marked xfail gaps — not a silent skip: (1) soak has no meaningful interior optimum in 5–15 d in this model, and (2) at the calibration-anchor diesel discount (0.15, mid of the U[0,0.30] range) the gross-margin-optimum slug size is below BGW-8's published 1,040–1,560 t — read as revealed preference, OIL's actual slug sizes argue its steam is cheaper than the mid-range (TIER1_PROGRESS_LOG.md §12.2). The rev-12 cold-well-pumpability xfail is RESOLVED, not carried forward: rev 13 makes the cold counterfactual obey the SAME float policy as the stimulated well, so at the base 45% formation water cut it is correctly SHUT IN (not "unpumpable yet producing") — see the cold-well note below. These tests assert behaviour the models must exhibit — monotonicity, bounds, conservation, limiting cases — and, in test_benchmarks.py, that the rev-13 twin reproduces published Baghewala and analogue-field numbers (SOR 3.0–4.6, 5–6× uplift, 15–40 bbl/d) UNDER THE DEFAULT pull policy (the calibration anchor), rather than fitting to data — under VFD-hold the reference SOR (3.46) and a 500-t slug (2.65) both miss those bands by a hair, pinned as findings, not re-specified.

    What has NOT been validated

    • No history match. No measured Baghewala production, temperature or dynamometer data has been compared against the twin. Every figure in this product is synthetic.
    • Diesel bulk discount is unsourced and load-bearing. Base moved 0.15 (rev 13, the mid of an unsourced U[0,0.30] range); 0.30 (bulk) and 0 (retail) are named presets. At full retail price every sensible cycle is gross-margin-loss-making, and at 0.15 the gross-margin-optimum slug size drops below BGW-8's published practice (the new xfail above) — this judgement call moves the headline by ₹5–10k/cycle-day, not just its sign.
    • Rod-string failure is not priced. Holding the float index at 0.6 for ~60 days/cycle under VFD-hold has a graded damage exposure (ΣFI³) about 5× the pull policy's, but the model has no rod-failure or workover cost term — so "float avoidance" cannot show up in ₹ anywhere on this dashboard. One OIL rod-failure/workover cost and frequency would close this (TIER1_PROGRESS_LOG.md §12.13/§12.15).
    • Fixed opex, ₹5,000/day [ASSUMPTION]. No per-well Indian onshore lifting-cost figure was found; cross-checked only against a rough $/bbl sense-check, not a cited source. It cancels in the incremental margin unless the cold well itself becomes uneconomic.
    • $10/bbl heavy-oil discount [ASSUMPTION]. No Baghewala-specific crude-marketing discount off OIL's blended FY25 realisation was found; applied as a flat haircut to both price-deck presets.
    • Royalty/cess: now a third deck, not netted from the other two. FY25 and $65 are both national pre-levy values; a THIRD deck (~₹3,600/bbl, rev 13) nets OIL's own royalty (20%) and OID cess (20%) off the FY25 base — on it, every feasible grid point is negative, even against a shut-in cold well. The 2018 ER-policy 50% cess waiver a qualifying CSS job might get is NOT netted.
    • fluid.formation_water_cut = 0.45 is an assumption, not a Baghewala measurement (no field datum, OIL_DATA_REQUEST item 2) — the native water cut of the reservoir liquid the water-cut STATE model (condensate flowback + formation blend) declines toward late in the cycle. It is the #4 driver of the recommendation's net-cash variance (rev 13, 1,500-draw tornado) — behind the cold heavy-oil viscosity, the cold well's own skin factor, and oil price.
    • The Pal–Rhodes emulsion law and the water-in-oil → oil-in-water inversion point (fluid.emulsion_inversion_wc) are taken from the PUBLISHED band, not fit to a Baghewala emulsion sample; the 10× relative-viscosity cap near inversion is an explicit [ASSUMPTION]. This law is what the whole rev-12 recommendation's mechanism (delayed float onset) rests on.
    • fluid.condensate_recovery_frac = 0.70 (fraction of injected steam mass produced back as condensate) is an [ASSUMPTION], not measured — the #2 UQ driver, since it sets WHEN the produced stream crosses the emulsion inversion.
    • reservoir.P_current_kPa defaults to the virgin pressure (no depletion) — Baghewala's actual current (post-38-cycle) reservoir pressure is unknown (top OIL data ask); the UQ band U[7.4, 9.4] MPa is a placeholder range, not a measurement.
    • Cold-well pumpability — resolved in rev 13, but still an upper bound. The cold counterfactual now obeys the SAME float policy as the stimulated well: at the base 45% formation water cut, its floating index at the policy floor exceeds the alarm line, so it is correctly SHUT IN (cash 0) under every policy except "none" — the old rev-12 "unpumpable yet producing" double standard is gone (the xfail is un-xfailed, a passing test). But Baghewala's wells WERE produced cold historically (~2–3 bbl/d), so every absolute incremental ₹ figure here is still an UPPER bound: a "pumpable" counterfactual (slowed to ~0.53 spm, as the field did) is carried in the model and reads ~₹11.4k/cycle-day LOWER at every set-point — the paired gain between two set-points does not depend on this choice. One measured cold-well dynamometer card would settle which counterfactual is right — see the cold-well-vs-shut-in note in §7.2 above.
    • Pump geometry is typical, not Baghewala-specific (1.75 in × 86 in class), same status as the plunger diameter and rod string in the field-parameters table above.
    • Net-pay vs gross-pay conflict. reservoir.thickness_m = 12 m is treated as net pay (GEOHORIZONS 2015); the problem statement's column suggests a gross thickness nearer 50 m. The twin uses the conservative (thinner, net) figure; the discrepancy is printed, not resolved silently — same policy as the API-gravity conflict below.
    • No uncertainty on model form. The hold-out R²/MAE figures quoted on the optimiser page are surrogate-vs-twin agreement only, not twin-vs-Baghewala agreement.
    • No SCADA link. The API contract (MOCK / API_BASE) is implemented and exercised, but nothing on these pages has ever been fed by a live instrument.

    Recalibration plan, given OIL data

    1. History-match the thermal model against measured injection and observation-well temperature, freeing the drainage radius and (now that BL_DELTA_FACTOR is sourced physics) fluid.formation_water_cut/fluid.condensate_recovery_frac instead.
    2. Replace the Walther anchor with lab viscosity–temperature points; set walther_A and walther_B in the parameter file.
    3. Get a sourced bulk-diesel price (or confirm the −30% discount), a sourced per-well opex figure, and a Baghewala-specific heavy-oil marketing discount — these three plus the oil-price deck are what the headline incremental-margin sign depends on.
    4. Re-run the Pres-depletion sensitivity against a wider set of analogue fields to further test the Darcy-band re-specification.
    5. Get a measured or assumed-typical water cut and pump geometry specific to Baghewala, and resolve the net-pay/gross-pay conflict with OIL.
    6. Regenerate the 600-cycle design of experiments (now incl. the float-policy dimension), retrain all surrogates, and republish R² and MAE alongside a residual plot against held-out twin runs.

    What this cannot do

    • It cannot predict an individual cycle at Baghewala to engineering accuracy. It predicts the direction and rough magnitude of the response to a set-point change.
    • It cannot size a pumping unit, specify a rod string or certify a mechanical design.
    • It cannot represent steam override, gravity segregation, channelling, formation damage, sand production or wellbore heat loss — none of which are in the model.
    • It cannot see a second well. All economics on the overview page are explicitly labelled “one well”.
    • It cannot be run against a controller. Staging set-points writes to session state; nothing is transmitted.
