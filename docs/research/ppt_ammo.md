# PPT Ammo — sourced numbers for slides

Each line: the number, a one-line context, and the source. (C) = CONFIRMED, (T) = TYPICAL/industry, (D) = derived from confirmed numbers.

1. **1991** — Year heavy oil was discovered at Baghewala (well A1), Bikaner-Nagaur basin, Rajasthan. (C) — [oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields](https://www.oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields)
2. **India's first CSS project** — OIL began India's first Cyclic Steam Stimulation at well BGW-8 in December 2018, with technical help from a Calgary firm (Belgrave Oil & Gas Corp.) — heavy-oil thermal EOR was essentially new to India before this. (C) — [Oil & Gas Journal](https://www.ogj.com/drilling-production/production-operations/unconventional-resources/article/17296851/oil-starts-css-of-well-in-rajasthan)
3. **14–17° API, 8,000–15,000 cP @ 50°C** — Baghewala's Jodhpur Sandstone crude: barely flows at reservoir temperature without heat. (C) — [SPE-23APOG-535203](https://onepetro.org/SPEAPOG/proceedings-abstract/23APOG/2-23APOG/535203)
4. **~90–100× more viscous than a "typical" 18° API crude** — Baghewala's oil (~11,500 cP @ 50°C) vs. a generic 18° API crude (~120 cP @ 50°C) — the field is a viscosity outlier even among heavy oils, which is exactly why CSS + downhole heating are needed. (D) — see `baghewala_facts.md` §6
5. **~1,150 m** — average depth of the Jodhpur Sandstone reservoir being produced via CSS + sucker-rod pumps. (C) — [oil-india.com](https://www.oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields); [SPE-23APOG-535203](https://onepetro.org/SPEAPOG/proceedings-abstract/23APOG/2-23APOG/535203)
6. **5–6×** — production increase observed after a CSS steam cycle at the Baghewala pilot well. (C) — [OGJ](https://www.ogj.com/drilling-production/production-operations/unconventional-resources/article/17296851/oil-starts-css-of-well-in-rajasthan); OIL internal PPT
7. **39 CSS cycles** completed at Baghewala as of June 2025; 19 wells CSS'd in FY2025-26 alone (~72% more than the year before). (C) — OIL internal PPT; [PSU Watch](https://psuwatch.com/newsupdates/oil-india-ramps-up-crude-production-from-rajasthans-thar-desert)
8. **218 t → 32,787 t → 43,773 t** — Baghewala's annual crude output growth: FY2016-17 → FY2024-25 → FY2025-26 — a ~200× rise since commercial CSS began. (C) — OIL internal PPT; PSU Watch
9. **~655–1,202 bbl/day** — current field output range reported across 2025–2026 sources, with the higher figure (a record) reported amid the 2026 Hormuz-crisis push for domestic crude. (C) — OIL internal PPT; [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/hormuz-blocked-india-turns-to-thar-desert-oil-india-ramps-up-crude-output-from-rajasthan-field-524088-2026-04-05)
10. **3–8 (typically ~6 lifecycle average)** — typical steam-oil ratio (SOR) for CSS worldwide; a cycle is considered thermally efficient if SOR < 3. This is the number the ML optimizer in this project is trying to beat. (T) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/cyclic-steam-stimulation)
11. **~3.0 GJ (~840 kWh) of fuel energy per tonne of steam** — calculated directly from Baghewala's own reported first-cycle numbers (3,100 kg/hr steam from 220 kg/hr diesel fuel), and consistent with generic industry figures (2.5–3.6 GJ/tonne for medium/high-pressure steam). (D) — see `baghewala_facts.md` §4
12. **$15,000–$50,000 per workover** — typical cost of a sucker-rod-pump failure (rig time + rod/tubing replacement + lost production); >50% of SRP failures trace to metal-on-metal rod/tubing wear, which is exactly the "floating index" risk this digital twin is built to flag before it happens. (T) — [iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/industries/oil-and-gas/ai-sucker-rod-pump-failure-prediction-mature-oil-wells)
13. **~550–600 km** — road distance from Baghewala (Jaisalmer district, Thar Desert) to Jaipur — remote-field logistics context for why real-time/remote digital-twin monitoring matters. (T, range varies 485–650 km by source/route) — [distancebetween2.com](https://distancebetween2.com/jaipur/jaisalmer)
14. **280–305°C, 85–97 kgf/cm²** — actual steam injection temperature and pressure used in Baghewala's first CSS cycle (well BGW-08) — far hotter/higher-pressure than the textbook ~250°C default, showing the twin is calibrated to real field practice, not just generic assumptions. (C) — OIL internal PPT
15. **<10% porosity, poor-to-fair reservoir quality** — the Jodhpur Sandstone is a genuinely hard reservoir to produce from (low porosity + extreme viscosity), which is the core justification for needing a digital twin to optimize CSS/SRP settings rather than relying on trial and error. (C) — [GEOHORIZONS Jan 2015](https://spgindia.org/geohorizons_vol_20_january_2015/technical_articles7_january_2015_30_12_14.pdf)
