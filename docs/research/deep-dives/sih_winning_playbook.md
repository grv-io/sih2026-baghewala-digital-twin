# SIH 2026 Winning Playbook — Team Baghewala (PS SIH26120, Oil India Limited)

Research compiled 13 Sep 2026. Every claim is tagged:

- **[CONFIRMED]** — official SIH 2026 Guidelines PDF, the official Idea Presentation template, or sih.gov.in pages. Source URL given.
- **[REPORTED]** — participant blogs, winner interviews, coaching sites, college circulars. Treat as pattern evidence, not rules.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** — circulating but with no traceable source. Explicitly flagged so you do not plan around it.

Two primary documents were downloaded and text-extracted directly rather than read second-hand: the **SIH 2026 Guidelines** (26 pp., PDF creation date 29 Jul 2026) and the **official SIH 2026 Idea Presentation Format** `.pptx` (7 slides, 13.333in × 7.5in). Anything sourced from those is CONFIRMED.

## 1. How SIH actually judges

### 1.1 The official pipeline [CONFIRMED]

(Source: https://sih-uit.vercel.app/assets/sih-2026-guidelines.pdf — the SIH 2026 Guidelines deck; also https://www.sih.gov.in/faqs)

1. **SPOC registration** — a senior faculty member registers the college on sih.gov.in; the SIH implementation team authorises the account. Opened July 2026, last date 31 Jul 2026.
2. **Internal hackathon** — mandatory. *"Only students selected in the Internal Hackathon can be registered for the Smart India Hackathon 2026."* The SPOC must upload an internal-hackathon report of **max 15 pages** containing: event overview (1p), event photos (2p), participating-team pictures (2p), judging process (1p), jury panel details (1p), nominated top teams (1p), judges' information (1p), total teams & students (2p), news articles (1p), social-media promotion (1p) with `#sih2026` and `#smartindiahackathon2026` tagging MIC's official handle.
3. **Nomination cap** — **max 50 teams per institute (45 shortlisted + 5 waitlisted)**; 100 per university. *"There is no bar for the Software and hardware categories"* — the split is the SPOC's discretion, only the total 50 binds. (Note: the widely repeated "45 = 30 software + 15 hardware" split is **[UNVERIFIED]** and contradicted by the official text.)
4. **Idea submission** — the team leader (credentials issued after the SPOC enters team details) submits: verified team name, verified college authorisation letter PDF, all 6 members' names/gender/email/mobile, chosen PS, **idea title, idea description, idea presentation (PDF)**.
5. **Screening** — online, deck-only. No presentation, no interview.
6. **Grand Finale** — offline at nodal centres across India, **proposed December 2026**.

### 1.2 Published evaluation criteria [CONFIRMED — but no weightages exist]

The guidelines state verbatim:

> *"Evaluation criteria will include novelty of the idea, complexity, clarity and details in the prescribed format, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, user experience and potential for future work progression."*

That is the **entire** published rubric. There are **no official percentage weightages** for the internal round, the screening round, or the finale.

**Warning:** several coaching blogs publish confident-looking tables (e.g. "Innovation 25% / Technical feasibility 20% / Impact 20% / Presentation 15%"). One such page (reskilll.com) also asserts a **10-slide** limit and a 10 MB cap — both **flatly contradicted by the official template**, which mandates six slides and PDF-only. Treat all such weightage tables as **[UNVERIFIED]** and do not optimise against them.

Note what *is* in the official list and is easy to under-serve: **"clarity and details in the prescribed format"** (i.e. template compliance is itself a scored criterion), **"sustainability"**, **"user experience"**, and **"potential for future work progression"**. A physics+ML digital twin deck naturally over-indexes on novelty/complexity and under-indexes on UX and post-hackathon continuation. Fix that deliberately.

### 1.3 Shortlisting economics [CONFIRMED]

- **500-idea cap per PS.** *"only 500 ideas will be submitted for a particular PS. Once the count number got all 500 ideas, the particular PS will get freeze."* Live counts are public on the portal, next to each PS.
- **4–5 teams per problem statement may be selected for the grand finale**, but *"the final decision rests with the problem statement creating organization, which isn't obligated to declare a winner unless student proposals meet their expectations."*
- One team may submit against **max 2 problem statements**.

**Live intelligence on SIH26120:** third-party scrapers of the portal showed **1–2 ideas submitted** against SIH26120 in early September 2026 (Source: https://zaidsayyed.in/tools/sih-problem-statements/theme/smart-automation, snapshot ~3 Sep 2026; a second scrape showed SIH26120=1, SIH26121=1, SIH26122=3). **[REPORTED — verify the live number on sih.gov.in before deciding.]** If it is still in single digits, this PS is one of the least contested on the board, and the binding constraint is almost certainly *not* beating other teams — it is **clearing Oil India's own bar**, since OIL may simply declare no winner. SIH 2025 did exactly that against three PS IDs (below).

### 1.4 Jury composition pattern

**[CONFIRMED]** The problem-statement-creating organisation holds final say on finale selection and prize award, and in a tie *"the final decision of the prize money distribution will be taken by the problem statement creator only."*

**[REPORTED]** Across 2019–2025 accounts, the observed pattern at the finale is a rotating panel per PS made of (a) the sponsoring organisation's own engineers/domain officers, (b) an academic, (c) an industry/technology evaluator. Participant accounts consistently describe the sponsor's engineers as the ones who ask the hard domain questions and whose scepticism must be overturned (see §3.1). Internal-round juries are college faculty, sometimes with a local industry guest.

### 1.5 Finale format, prizes, IP [CONFIRMED unless marked]

- **Prize: ₹1,50,000 per problem statement.** *"There would be ONE single winning team for every problem statement... The prize money will be given by the collaborating ministry/industry ONLY IF that organization likes the idea of the winning team."*
- **SIH 2025 outcome data:** 322 winning entries; the results page explicitly states *"there are no winners against the PS IDs - SIH25056, SIH25199, SIH25214."* Joint winners split ₹75,000 each. Additional awards observed: Consolation ₹15,000, Girls Achiever ₹20,000, Future Innovators ₹10,000. (Source: https://www.sih.gov.in/sih2025/sih2025-grand-finale-result)
- **Duration [REPORTED]:** software edition = **36 continuous hours**; hardware edition ≈ 5 days. Not stated in the 2026 guidelines PDF, but consistent across every participant account and every 2026 preview article.
- **Mentors:** up to 2, from industry or academia, ≥5 years' experience, **optional**. Responsibilities are explicitly to help *"convert students' ideas into a working prototype"* before the finale and *"to work collaboratively with the allotted student team during the SIH Grand Finale."*
- **Logistics:** travel + accommodation organised, but **reimbursement ceiling ₹3,000/person** round trip against actual bills. College stamped photo ID and consent letter are **mandatory** at the venue. No non-registered person (faculty, family, friend) may be at the venue.
- **IP:** *"Intellectual property (IP) of the winning idea would be split equally between industry that gave the problem statements and the winning team or will be decided on the mutual agreement."* Read this before you sign anything at the venue.
- **Originality clause:** *"The ideas or solutions provided/developed/proposed by the teams must be new and must not have been present in any previous event/program of any sort."* Relevant if any part of the Baghewala twin was shown at a prior college event.

## 2. Why teams die at the internal round

Documented/official causes first, then reported.

**[CONFIRMED — structural disqualifiers]**
1. Team is not exactly 6 members, or has no female member, or mixes colleges.
2. Team name contains the institute's name, or is not unique.
3. Missing/incorrectly formatted college authorisation letter (letterhead, all 6 names, up to 2 mentors, principal/dean/director signature, college seal).
4. Deck not in the prescribed template, or over six slides, or uploaded as PPT/DOCX. *"No PPT, Word Doc or any other format will be supported."*
5. Team details, once entered by the SPOC, **cannot be altered** — a typo in a member's email is permanent.
6. Missing the SPOC's internal deadline: the SPOC must finish nomination *and* the report before the portal deadline, so college cut-offs land days-to-weeks earlier than the national one.

**[REPORTED — judging causes]** (Sources: https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026-internal-hackathon-guide, https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026, https://blogs.reskilll.com/sih-2026-internal-hackathon-how-colleges-select-teams-get-nominated/)

7. **Walls of text copied from Wikipedia or ChatGPT** — the single most cited reason. Generic decks where you could swap the ministry/state name and every slide still reads true.
8. **Overpromising**: "AI + blockchain + IoT" in 36 hours. Faculty read this as not having costed the work.
9. **One person talks, five stand silent.** Reported as an instant credibility kill; juries deliberately ask a non-leader a question.
10. **Defensive answers.** Admitting an unknown scores better than guessing.
11. **No demo of any kind** — not even a clickable mockup — while a competing team has one.
12. **Feasibility slide that lists only benefits** with no risks and no mitigations.
13. **References that say "government datasets"** instead of naming a source and a URL.
14. **Theme/category mismatch** with what the portal actually lists for that PS.

One coaching source estimates **>60% of campus teams are cut at internals** and that "80% of teams are eliminated before writing finale code" for process reasons rather than technical ones. **[UNVERIFIED — no official elimination statistics are published.]** Directionally, the 50-team institute cap makes a steep internal cut arithmetically certain at any large college.

## 3. What winners consistently did

Ten accounts reviewed. The convergent patterns matter more than any single story.

### 3.1 The iterate-with-the-jury loop — the single most repeated pattern

- **Team TwinX, SIH 2025 winner** (KK Wagh IEER, Nashik; MathWorks PS; road-network digital twin — closest analogue to our PS). Their stated rule: *"get feedback from judges in mentoring round and modify the solution till upcoming evaluation round."* They worked **34–35 of the 36 hours** and *"stayed focused and motivated to complete every task given by the judges."* Timeline: Aug–Sep internal rounds focused on **team cohesion, "identifying and then playing on strength of each member," not on perfecting the solution**; October refining after submission; November mentorship with experts on *"refining the concept, improving usability, and balancing technical depth."* (Source: https://blogs.mathworks.com/student-lounge/2026/04/06/from-real-roads-to-real-simulations-how-team-twinx-won-smart-india-hackathon-2025/)
- **HUL supply-chain team, 2019 winner** — the clearest anecdote of the arc. Round 1: company judges *"extremely skeptical"*; the team was *"heartbroken."* Round 2: judges said scanning QR codes in bulk is impossible; the author replied *"I told them it is possible"* and pivoted to an ML-based scanner. Round 3: judges were *"surprised"* by the working model and then told them to focus on *"the business side"* and that *"the presentation in the power round matters a lot."* Four stages, the last being a **"power round"** final pitch. The author also admits butchering the very first pitch: *"I completely butcher the pitch and failed to explain anything properly"* — and still won, because rounds 2 and 3 showed delta. (Source: https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/contest-experiences/smart-india-hackathon-experience-2/)
- **[REPORTED] Evaluator behaviour**: evaluators visit the same table **3–4 times** across the 36 hours and *"are comparing against their own last note."* The scored quantity is **visible advancement between visits**, not one polished final demo. (Source: https://zaidsayyed.in/blog/sih-2026)
- **[REPORTED]** Judges may demand a pivot that seems impossible; the point is to *"test perseverance"* and to see progress, not literal compliance. (Aggregated from SIH 2024 winner accounts.)

### 3.2 Prototype readiness before the finale

- **Team Solar Masters, SIH 2024 winner** (SPSU Udaipur, PS SIH1731, MathWorks): they **reused prior work** — *"Our team had already worked on a solar tracking system for a college-level hackathon, but due to lack of knowledge and resources, we could not fully develop it."* Before the finale they took MathWorks self-paced courses, **requested and got licensed software access from the sponsor**, built the software prototype in Simscape, and attended a **sponsor webinar for finalists** on optimisation and presentation. At the finale they demoed three things in sequence: simulation results → live hardware tracking → monitoring app. (Source: https://blogs.mathworks.com/student-lounge/2025/06/13/innovation-meets-excellence-solar-masters-winning-journey-at-smart-india-hackathon-2024/)
  - **Lesson for us:** ask Oil India, via the SPOC/mentor route, for *anything* — a data dictionary, a sample SCADA export, a dynamometer card set, a well-test sheet. Sponsor engagement before the finale is a repeated winner behaviour.
- **[REPORTED]** SIH 2024 winners: *"had already built so much together before meeting in person for the first time at the internal hackathon"*; after shortlisting they *"fine-tuned their prototype, polished their PPT and documentation, and rehearsed their pitch."*
- **[REPORTED]** *"Before two hours of final evaluation, stop making major changes, get together to test and deploy your product and debug, because judges would use the application thoroughly."* Judges **drive the app themselves**. Design your dashboard so a stranger cannot break it in 90 seconds.

### 3.3 Demo, roles, storytelling

- **[REPORTED]** *"Demo first, sixty seconds, on the thing that actually works. Slides only if they ask."*
- **[REPORTED]** Present like a startup: problem → solution → impact → scalability, in 5 minutes.
- **[REPORTED]** Role split that survives jury probing: domain researcher, documentation/slide owner, demo operator (owns runtime stability), frontend, backend, model specialist. **Each member owns and can defend one module.**
- **[REPORTED]** Name one specific person who has the problem, not "users." For us: the field operator at Baghewala deciding today's steam soak schedule.
- **[REPORTED]** Winners rehearse a 3-minute pitch **with a stopwatch** the night before internals.
- **[REPORTED]** Team TwinX sketched a handmade trophy the week before finals *"not to predict the outcome, but to keep ourselves motivated and focused"* — morale infrastructure is a real 36-hour concern.

## 4. PSU / org-sponsored PS dynamics (our case: Oil India)

**[CONFIRMED]** The sponsoring organisation controls: shortlisting for the finale (4–5 teams/PS), whether to declare a winner at all, tie-break distribution, and half the IP of the winning idea. SIH 2025 shows organisations do exercise the no-winner option (three PS IDs with zero winners).

**[REPORTED]** What org/ministry evaluators reportedly probe, distilled across accounts:

- **Ownership clarity** — *"Name the department and role running it day-to-day."* Not "the company"; a named function.
- **Pilot scoping** — they are *"mentally costing a pilot, not a platform."* One field, a handful of wells, a defined number of users. *"A team that can scope one sounds like a team that has shipped before."*
- **Integration** — *"Which of our existing systems would this talk to?"* Name real ones.
- **Cost to run** — *"What does this cost to run for a year at scale?"* with rough line items.
- **Data governance** — where data lives, who accesses it, anonymisation, applicable law.
- **Failure in the field** — *"What breaks in the field?"* Answer with **three real failure modes plus mitigation**, not reassurance.
- **AI attribution** — *"How much of this did AI write?"* Honest answers reportedly score better.
- **Adoption** — training time for the end user; why previous attempts failed.

**[UNVERIFIED]** Internships/PPOs/MoUs to SIH winners are widely referenced in general terms ("hackathons are becoming gateways to internships, pre-placement interviews"), and the guidelines do say *"Selected ideas will be supported to be developed further so that industries/government institutions will be able to utilize these ideas."* But **no specific Oil India or ONGC SIH internship/MoU outcome could be located.** No petroleum-PSU winners appear in the SIH 2025 results list at all (nearest energy entries: Power Grid Corporation under Ministry of Power; Hindustan Copper and NIRM under Ministry of Mines; SAIL/NMDC under Ministry of Steel). Do not promise the team an OIL internship — treat it as upside.

**Oil India's SIH 2026 slate**, all Smart Automation / Software: **SIH26120** (our Baghewala CSS+SRP digital twin), **SIH26121** (eRTMAC-NWIS, offset-well knowledge and decision support), **SIH26122** (intelligent data capture & schedule-linking for infrastructure projects). Three PS from one PSU signals a live internal digital push at OIL — worth one line in the deck.

## 5. Finale playbook for our team (36 hours)

We already have physics + ML + dashboard running. That inverts the normal problem: our risk is not "can't build in time," it is **"looks pre-built, shows no delta between rounds."** Plan against that.

**Pre-build (before travelling):** the full pipeline, seeded and stable; a one-command local start with no internet dependency; a recorded 90-second screen capture of the happy path on a pen drive; the PPT and a PDF of it offline; a printed one-pager of the architecture; the college photo ID + consent letter + authorisation letter (hard blockers at the gate).

**Deliberately hold back three things to build *there*** — one per evaluation round, so each visit has a visible delta:
1. **Round 1 → 2:** a jury-requested scenario. Arrive with the twin able to run a scenario you have *not* pre-baked (e.g. change soak duration / steam quality / rod-string load) and add it live.
2. **Round 2 → 3:** the **recalibrate-on-real-data showpiece** (below).
3. **Round 3 → power round:** the deployability layer — operator-facing view, alerting, export/handover artefacts, the one-year cost line items.

**The recalibrate-on-real-data moment — make it a scheduled act, not an accident.** Keep a held-out well/cycle sealed. When an OIL engineer visits, ask them for a plausible field parameter or hand them the held-out slice; recalibrate the twin in front of them and show the error metric drop, before/after, on screen. Then say the honest thing: what the model still cannot see. Two winner accounts turned exactly this arc — sceptical engineer → demonstrated capability → convinced engineer — into the win. Rehearse it to under three minutes and make sure it cannot fail (fixed seed, pre-validated data slice, a fallback pre-computed result you can show if the live run stalls).

**Mentor-round talking points** (they will decide whether to keep investing attention in us):
- One sentence on the physics: what the CSS thermal model and the SRP model each compute, and why coupling them well-to-surface is the actual contribution.
- Where ML sits: surrogate/correction on top of physics, not a black box replacing it. Say this early; PSU engineers distrust pure-ML claims about reservoirs.
- The honest uncertainty statement: what we calibrated on, what we extrapolate, what error band we claim.
- One number: barrels, steam cost, or rod-failure interventions avoided per well per year — with the arithmetic shown.
- The pilot ask: *N* wells at Baghewala, *X* weeks, this data feed, this named role operating it.

**Mid-eval checkpoints** — at each visit, open with *"Since you were here, we changed three things"* and name them. Close with *"what would you like to see next time?"* and write it down in front of them. Bring that literal list to the next round.

**Demo-failure contingencies:**
- Laptop dies / venue power: second laptop with an identical clone, plus phone hotspot; nothing may require internet.
- Live run hangs: pre-computed result screens in a folder, plus the 90-second recording. Never debug in front of a jury for more than 20 seconds — switch to the recording, keep talking, fix it after they leave.
- Dashboard breaks: keep static PNG exports of every key chart.
- The "judges drive it themselves" risk: input validation on every field, and no state that a wrong click can corrupt.
- The pivot demand: agree, scope it to something achievable in the gap, deliver a partial version by the next round, and say what you traded away. Refusing outright is the reported failure mode.

**Roles for six:** demo operator (owns runtime, never codes during a jury visit), physics owner, ML owner, dashboard owner, domain/deployment owner (answers cost, integration, field failure), narrator/documentation owner (opens and closes every round, tracks jury asks). Every member must be able to answer one question about their module without deferring to the leader.

## 6. Internal-round checklist, dated backward from 30 Sep 2026

**Deadline conflict — resolve with your SPOC today.** The official SIH 2026 Guidelines PDF (created 29 Jul 2026) states the last date for team nomination and idea submission is **15 Sep 2026**. Multiple September 2026 sources and college circulars now state **30 Sep 2026**, and colleges are running internal rounds on 9–15 Sep — which is only coherent under the later date. Plan to **30 Sep**, but **confirm in writing with the MNIT SPOC**, and treat the SPOC's own cut-off (always earlier) as the real deadline.

**Is there a 2026 idea-video requirement?** **No.** The official 2026 idea-submission field list is: team name, authorisation letter, member details, PS/SIC, idea title, idea description, idea presentation (PDF). **No video is required from teams.** The only video in the guidelines is the **SPOC's** *"Internal Hackathon Image/Video Link"* for the college's own report.
