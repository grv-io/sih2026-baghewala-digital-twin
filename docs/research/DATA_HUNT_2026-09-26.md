  Source: Raju, S.V. et al. (inferred), "Geochemical characterization of neoproterozoic heavy oil from Rajasthan, India: Implications for future exploration of hydrocarbons" — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287256850) (full text not retrievable this session, 403).
- **Important discrepancy flagged**: a separate peer-reviewed *Scientific Reports* (Nature) paper gives **Baghewala-1 reservoir porosities of 16–25% (Jodhpur sandstone), 7–15% (Bilara dolostone), 3–12% (Hanseran evaporite)** — this is notably HIGHER than the existing facts.md figure of "<10%" sourced from GEOHORIZONS 2015 (Basha et al.). Also gives formation thicknesses: **Jodhpur Fm ~50 m, Bilara Dolomite ~40 m, Hanseran Evaporite ~170 m**, and source-rock **TOC 5–6%** for the Bilara–Hanseran sequence. This is a genuinely new, citable, peer-reviewed number for the model's `thickness_m` gap (Jodhpur Fm gross thickness ~50 m — though net *pay* thickness within that 50 m gross interval is still not separately stated, so the existing 12 m net-pay placeholder isn't directly replaced, but 50 m gross is a useful upper bound / cross-check). **Recommend the team reconcile the porosity conflict (16-25% vs <10%) before treating either as final** — possible explanation: Basha et al. 2015 may report *effective* porosity in the heavy-oil-bearing interval specifically, while this paper reports gross log porosity across the whole formation including non-pay streaks.
  Source: Yasin, Q., Baklouti, S., Sohail, G.M., Asif, M., Xufei, G. (2022). "Evaluation of Neoproterozoic source rock potential in SE Pakistan and adjacent Bikaner–Nagaur Basin India." *Scientific Reports* 12, 11086. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-14831-5 — [PMC9247107](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9247107/) (open access, successfully fetched). **[PEER-REVIEWED — high confidence, open access]**

- Nothing found met the bar of "openly downloadable and <50MB with a clear license" this session.

## What this lets us do

**(a) Numbers that can now calibrate/validate the physics, mapped to model quantities:**

1. **Reservoir porosity / thickness** (`reservoir.porosity`, `reservoir.thickness_m`) — the new Scientific Reports source (§6) gives Jodhpur Sandstone porosity 16–25% and gross formation thickness ~50 m at Baghewala-1 itself. This DIRECTLY conflicts with the current `field_params_recommended.json` value of porosity=0.09 (sourced from GEOHORIZONS 2015's "<10%"). **Action needed, not a free calibration win**: the team should decide which source to trust (or average/bracket both) before the SIH deck — flag both figures with the discrepancy noted, or dig for the reconciliation. The 50 m gross thickness is at least a useful upper-bound cross-check for `thickness_m` (currently 12 m placeholder, unconfirmed either way).
2. **μ(T) low-temperature sanity check** (`fluid.andrade_A`/`andrade_B_K`) — Cold Lake bitumen's 138,000 cP @ 15 °C (10° API) is a real public data point from a heavier, colder analog. It can be used as a *plausibility bound*, not a direct fit point (different oil, different API) — e.g., checking that the twin's fitted Andrade curve doesn't predict something absurdly lower than 138,000 cP at 15 °C for an oil that's lighter (15.5° API) than Cold Lake's.
3. **SOR / cycle length / cold rate** — still **no new Baghewala-specific numbers** found this session beyond what's already in facts.md (BGW-08 first-cycle injection rate, soak fraction, steam quality/T/P). The literature SOR benchmarks gathered (Qarn Alam's OSR figure — UNVERIFIED, do not use as-is) did not pan out as citable. **No change to the model's SOR range assumption is justified by this session's findings.**
4. **P_res response** — nothing new found; the existing hydrostatic-gradient cross-check in facts.md remains the best available consistency check.
5. **HSD/steam cost** — a rough retail-diesel-price-based upper bound (~₹8,200–8,300/tonne steam) can be added as a conservative cost-slide number, clearly labeled as a retail-price-based ceiling, not OIL's actual bulk cost.
