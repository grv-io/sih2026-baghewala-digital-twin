# Baghewala Field — Facts & Sources

Legend: **CONFIRMED** = found stated in a cited source. **TYPICAL** = industry-typical assumption used to fill a gap (no Baghewala-specific source found). **DERIVED** = calculated from two or more CONFIRMED numbers in this doc.

## 1. Location, discovery, operator

- **CONFIRMED** — Baghewala field is in the Bikaner-Nagaur sub-basin of the Rajasthan basin (also referred to in company material as part of "Jaisalmer" field operations); operated by Oil India Limited (OIL). Heavy oil discovered in 1991, at well Baghewala-1 (A1), in the early-Cambrian (Infra-Cambrian) Jodhpur Sandstone, with an initial DST gravity of ~19.5–20° API.
  Sources: [Oil India — Rajasthan Fields](https://www.oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields); [Basha et al., "Rock physics modeling and simultaneous inversion to map heavy-oil bearing sands in Baghewala area, Bikaner-Nagaur basin, India", GEOHORIZONS Jan 2015](https://spgindia.org/geohorizons_vol_20_january_2015/technical_articles7_january_2015_30_12_14.pdf)
- **CONFIRMED** — PML area ~200.26 sq km. Field also produces from a secondary Upper Carbonate (Bilara) zone, which is heavier and currently shut-in.
  Sources: same as above; [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/oil-ramps-up-crude-production-from-rajasthan-s-thar-amid-energy-crisis-126040500173_1.html)
- **CONFIRMED** — Crude is trucked from Baghewala (Jaisalmer district) to ONGC facilities at Mehsana, Gujarat, then piped to the IOCL Koyali refinery.
  Source: [Oil India — Rajasthan Fields](https://www.oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields)

## 2. Reservoir (Jodhpur Sandstone — primary producing zone)

- **CONFIRMED** — Average depth ~1,100–1,150 m.
  Sources: [Oil India — Rajasthan Fields](https://www.oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields); [SPE-23APOG "Case Study for Enhancement of Production of Heavy and Highly Viscous Crude Oil Using Electrical Downhole Heater" (2023)](https://onepetro.org/SPEAPOG/proceedings-abstract/23APOG/2-23APOG/535203); OIL internal presentation ["Baghewala PPT, Oil India Limited, 12.07.2025"](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/baghewala-ppt-oil-india-limited-12-07-2025-pptx/282258575) (secondary/uploaded copy — treat as best-available but not a primary-published source)
- **CONFIRMED** — API gravity 14–17° for the current producing crude (two independent sources agree). Note: the original 1991 discovery-test API at well A1 was ~19.5–20°; a broader "14–18° API" figure also appears in secondary write-ups. Recommend using 14–17° as the best-supported current value.
  Sources: SPE-23APOG-535203 (above); OIL internal PPT (above)
- **CONFIRMED** — Viscosity 8,000–15,000 cP at 50 °C (SPE paper) / 10,000–13,000 cP at 50 °C (OIL PPT). Both sources agree the oil is extremely viscous for its API gravity — much more viscous than a generic ~18° API crude (see §5), likely due to high wax/asphaltene content typical of Cambrian Jodhpur sand oils.
  Sources: SPE-23APOG-535203; OIL internal PPT
- **CONFIRMED** — Bottom-hole temperature ~50 °C; reservoir pressure ~116 kgf/cm² (≈ 11,400 kPa). Pressure is consistent with a near-hydrostatic gradient (~0.1 kgf/cm² per metre × 1,150 m ≈ 115 kgf/cm²), which is a good internal-consistency check.
  Source: OIL internal PPT (above)
- **CONFIRMED** — Porosity <10% (poor to fair reservoir quality); significant vertical/lateral heterogeneity.
  Source: GEOHORIZONS Jan 2015 (Basha et al., above)
- Net sand thickness — **NOT FOUND** in any public source searched. **TYPICAL**: kept spec default 12 m as an unconfirmed placeholder; typical Jodhpur-sand net pay in published cross-sections looks thin (a few to ~15 m) but no citable number was found.
- Rock thermal conductivity / heat capacity — **NOT FOUND** field-specific. **TYPICAL**: standard sandstone literature values (k ≈ 2–3 W/m·K, rock heat capacity ≈ 2.0–2.5×10⁶ J/m³K) — consistent with the spec defaults, kept as-is.

## 3. Reservoir (Upper Carbonate — secondary zone, shut-in)

- **CONFIRMED** — Average depth ~650 m; BHT ~42 °C; reservoir pressure ~64 kgf/cm² (≈6,280 kPa); API ~8.6–9.8°; viscosity ~59,000 cP at 50 °C. Currently shut-in (not the primary CSS target).
  Source: OIL internal PPT (above)

## 4. CSS pilot / operations history

- **CONFIRMED** — OIL first trialed steam-related heating around 2006 (chemical floods + steam from portable/mobile generators). India's first formal Cyclic Steam Stimulation (CSS) started December 2018 at well BGW-8, with technical assistance from Belgrave Oil & Gas Corp. (Calgary, Canada). Commercial-scale production dates from 2017.
  Sources: [Oil & Gas Journal — "OIL starts CSS of well in Rajasthan"](https://www.ogj.com/drilling-production/production-operations/unconventional-resources/article/17296851/oil-starts-css-of-well-in-rajasthan); [Oil India — Rajasthan Fields](https://www.oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields)
- **CONFIRMED** — Pilot well(s) showed a 5–6× increase in production after steam injection. 39 CSS cycles completed as of June 2025. OIL has since completed CSS on 19 wells in FY2025-26, ~72% more than the prior year, alongside 13 new wells drilled (up from 9).
  Sources: [Scribd — "CSS Technology Breakthrough in Baghewala"](https://www.scribd.com/document/444099090/CSS-Implementation-Baghewala-Rajasthan-Project); OIL internal PPT; [PSU Watch](https://psuwatch.com/newsupdates/oil-india-ramps-up-crude-production-from-rajasthans-thar-desert)
- **CONFIRMED — first CSS cycle parameters (well BGW-08)**:
  - Injection period: 14–21 days
  - Steam injection rate: ~3,100 kg/hr
  - Steam (wellhead) pressure: 85–97 kgf/cm²
  - Steam temperature: 280–305 °C
  - Steam quality: 60–70%
  - Water consumption: ~5,500 kg/hr (feedwater into the generator, exceeds steam output — consistent with quality <100%)
  - HSD (high-speed diesel) fuel consumption: ~220 kg/hr
  - Soak period: 50–60% of the injection-phase length (≈7–13 days for a 14–21 day injection)
  Source: OIL internal PPT (above) — this is the single richest technical source found; treat as CONFIRMED but note it is a secondary/uploaded copy of an internal deck, not an independently peer-reviewed publication.
- **DERIVED** — Fuel intensity of steam generation: 220 kg HSD / 3,100 kg steam ≈ 71 kg diesel per tonne of steam. At diesel LHV ≈ 42.6 MJ/kg, that's ≈ 3.0 GJ (≈ 840 kWh) of fuel energy per tonne of steam generated — consistent with generic industry figures for medium/high-pressure steam generation (see §5).
- Steam-oil ratio (SOR) actually achieved at Baghewala — **NOT FOUND** as an explicit reported number in any source searched.
- **Downhole electric heater (EDH) trial**: OIL/SPE trialed an electrical downhole heater in a horizontal well at Baghewala as a complement/alternative to CSS, aiming to extract oil from ~1,100 m depth; thermal methods outperformed non-thermal alternatives for this fluid.
  Sources: [SPE-23APOG-535203](https://onepetro.org/SPEAPOG/proceedings-abstract/23APOG/2-23APOG/535203); [SPE-24OPES "Using Downhole Electric Heaters to Complement or Replace CSS Operations"](https://onepetro.org/SPEOGWA/proceedings-abstract/24OPES/24OPES/D031S035R002/544525)
- **CONFIRMED** — Other technologies deployed: fishbone drilling (well BGW#40, first in an Indian heavy-oil reservoir), barefoot completions, vacuum-insulated tubing, mobile steam generators, and a documented rig-less fishing operation to recover a stuck downhole sucker-rod pump.
  Sources: [Oil India — Rajasthan Fields](https://www.oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields); [ResearchGate — "Successful Rig-Less Fishing Operation to Recover a Downhole Sucker Rod Pump at Baghewala Heavy Oil Field"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398187378_Successful_Rig-Less_Fishing_Operation_to_Recover_a_Downhole_Sucker_Rod_Pump_at_Baghewala_Heavy_Oil_Field_Rajasthan_-_India_A_Case_Study) (title/abstract only — full text was behind a login wall and could not be fetched this session)

## 5. Production and well count

- **CONFIRMED** — Crude output: ~655 bbl/d as of 10 July 2025 (OIL PPT); a subsequent 2026 news cycle reports a record 1,202 bbl/d from the Jodhpur sandstone, up ~70% from 705 bbl/d the year before (amid a wider Hormuz-crisis production push).
  Sources: OIL internal PPT; [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/hormuz-blocked-india-turns-to-thar-desert-oil-india-ramps-up-crude-output-from-rajasthan-field-524088-2026-04-05); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/oil-ramps-up-crude-production-from-rajasthan-s-thar-amid-energy-crisis-126040500173_1.html)
- **CONFIRMED** — Annual crude output grew from 218 tonnes (FY2016-17) to 32,787 tonnes (FY2024-25) to 43,773 tonnes (FY2025-26).
  Sources: OIL internal PPT; PSU Watch (above)
- **CONFIRMED (approximate, snapshot-dependent)** — Well counts vary by reporting date: 56 drilled / 34 producing (OIL company page); 52 drilled / 33 operational (2026 news roundup); well status breakdown as of April 2025: 25 flowing, 9 non-flowing, 3 shut-in, 4 under drilling/testing/CSS, 3 temporarily abandoned.
  Sources: [Oil India — Rajasthan Fields](https://www.oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields); [DiscoveryAlert roundup](https://discoveryalert.com.au/oil-india-crude-production-rajasthan-transformation-2026/); OIL internal PPT
- **CONFIRMED** — Artificial lift is by sucker rod pump (SRP) in the Baghewala wells; thermal completions use vacuum-insulated tubing to limit heat loss.
  Source: [Oil India — Rajasthan Fields](https://www.oil-india.com/rajasthan-fields)
- No Baghewala-specific SRP mechanical spec (stroke length, SPM, plunger diameter, rod grade) was found in any public source. **TYPICAL** values from general heavy-oil SRP practice are used in `field_params_recommended.json` (see that file's `_sources`).

## 6. TYPICAL industry values used to fill gaps (not Baghewala-specific)

- **TYPICAL** — CSS steam-oil ratio (SOR): commonly cited range is ~3–8 over a well's producing life (average ~6); a cycle/process is generally considered thermally efficient if SOR < 3. The SPEC's assumed 2–6 range is on the optimistic side of the literature but not unreasonable for an efficient cycle.
  Sources: [ScienceDirect — Cyclic Steam Stimulation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/cyclic-steam-stimulation); [Wikipedia — Steam to oil ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_to_oil_ratio)
- **TYPICAL** — Energy to generate steam: ~2.6–4 GJ/tonne (≈720–1,200 kWh/tonne) depending on pressure/temperature and boiler efficiency; for medium/high-pressure (10–100 bar) superheated steam specifically, ~2.5–3.2 GJ/tonne thermal, ~2.8–3.6 GJ/tonne fuel input. This matches the ~3.0 GJ/tonne figure derived from Baghewala's own HSD/steam ratio in §4.
  Source: [Thundersaid Energy — energy needed to produce steam](https://thundersaidenergy.com/downloads/energy-needed-to-produce-steam-enthalpy-and-entropy-data/)
- **TYPICAL** — Sucker rod pump workover cost after a failure: roughly $15,000–$50,000 per event (rig time, tubing/rod replacement, lost production), with >50% of failures from metal-to-metal rod/tubing wear.
  Source: [iFactory — AI for Sucker Rod Pump Failure Prediction](https://ifactoryapp.com/industries/oil-and-gas/ai-sucker-rod-pump-failure-prediction-mature-oil-wells)
- **TYPICAL** — Viscosity-temperature behavior for a *generic* ~18° API crude (NOT Baghewala's own oil, shown for contrast): ~250 cSt at 40 °C, ~130 cSt at 50 °C, ~79 cSt at 60 °C, ~18 cSt at 100 °C, ~8 cSt at 135 °C. Converting via density (≈0.947 g/cc for 18° API) gives ~123 cP at 50 °C — roughly 90–100× LESS viscous than Baghewala's confirmed 10,000–13,000 cP at 50 °C. This confirms Baghewala's crude is anomalously viscous for its API and should NOT be modeled with a generic API-gravity-to-viscosity correlation; use the field's own confirmed mu-T anchor instead.
  Source: [EngineeringToolBox — Crude Oil Viscosity vs. Gravity](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/crude-oil-petroleum-viscosity-gravity-density-d_1959.html)
- **TYPICAL** — Distance Baghewala (Jaisalmer district) to Jaipur: road distance figures across sources range ~485–650 km depending on route/tool; a representative "~550–600 km" is safe to cite on a slide without over-precision.
  Sources: [distancebetween2.com](https://distancebetween2.com/jaipur/jaisalmer); [Yatra](https://www.yatra.com/distance-between/distance-from-jaisalmer-to-jaipur.html)

## 7. Key caveat on source quality

The single richest technical source (steam rates, quality, pressures, temperatures, well-status breakdown) is a slide deck titled "Baghewala PPT oil india limited 12.07.2025.pptx" hosted on SlideShare — apparently an internal OIL operations presentation that has been uploaded publicly. Its numbers are internally consistent (e.g., reservoir pressure matches a hydrostatic gradient check) and consistent with the independently-published SPE paper (API 14–17°, viscosity 8,000–15,000 cP at 50 °C, depth ~1,100 m), which increases confidence. It is still a secondary/unverified copy rather than a company-published report, so it is flagged as CONFIRMED-but-secondary throughout this document rather than being cited alone for any critical number.
